What I do four fields, one chain

Trained first as an engineer in computer science and statistics, then in the optimisation of athletic performance. I work at the exact boundary between information-systems architecture and elite sport science, a place where you need to write the pipeline and know what the measurement means.

Data governance

Mapping athlete data flows, writing the data dictionaries and setting the rules that keep a federation's data estate coherent. Without that work, every coaching staff recomputes its own indicator and nothing can be compared to anything.

SQLData modellingDocumentation

Pipelines & sensors

Building the API connectors and ETL chains that bring heterogeneous feeds (GPS, LPS, tracking, force plates) into a single reference store, with the quality controls that have to come with them.

PythonPandasREST APIs

Modelling the effort

Quantifying training load, reading force–velocity mechanical profiles, and estimating a young athlete's potential while accounting for relative, biological and training age. Raw performance conflates those three variables every single time.

Scikit-learnRStatistics

Decision support

Making all of it usable by a coaching staff on a Monday morning: dashboards, visual reporting, and training the people who rely on them. An indicator nobody reads is worth nothing.

Power BIStreamlitR Shiny

NCAA scouting — published models and an evaluated AI agent

A pipeline of 116,000 player-seasons and 530,000 Division I plays, shot charts, momentum and a draft model frozen before the 2026 draft (9 of its top 10 drafted, AUC 0.97). An AI agent writes scouting reports through tool calls: structured outputs, guardrails, checked numbers, and a benchmark of four models.

Development curves of NCAA players

A Bayesian hierarchical model of how Division I players develop, fitted on 34,670 seasons. Half of the gain comes between the first and second year; conferences differ in level, not in development speed, which stays individual. The report also shows where the models disagree, and why.

Open source RbrmsStanQuarto Report Code

Football tracking analysis

25 Hz tracking from two professional matches: running load, accelerations and decelerations, in-match acceleration–speed profiles and worst-case demands. Mechanical and metabolic power with every equation written out: with no tuning, it recovers the published values (39 % anaerobic share, 40 % published). Plus a vectorised pitch-control model.
