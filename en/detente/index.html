Lab try it with your phone

How high can you jump?

At INSEP, I measure jumps on force plates. Your phone doesn't have one, but it has an accelerometer: jump with it, it measures your flight time and works out your height. Then compare yourself with pro basketball players.

Everything happens on your phone: nothing is sent or stored.

  1. Hold the phone flat against your chest, arms crossed over it. Your arms shouldn't help.
  2. Tap “Start”, wait for “Jump!” (and the vibration).
  3. Jump once, as high as you can, and land on the balls of your feet, legs straight.

Stable floor, no socks on parquet, and hold the phone tight: screens are not covered by this website.

0cm

      Benchmarks: published orders of magnitude for a countermovement jump, hands on chest. Phone accuracy: about ± 2 cm (60 to 100 readings per second, against often 1,000 on a force plate).

      Under the hood high-school physics and a bit of signal processing

      1. Listen

      The phone reports its acceleration 60 to 100 times per second, gravity included. Standing still, it reads 1 g; during push-off, over 2 g.

      2. Find the flight

      In the air, the phone is in free fall: its accelerometer reads almost 0 g. Take-off and landing are the two crossings of a low threshold, placed to the millisecond by interpolating between two readings.

      3. Work out the height

      Going up and coming down takes a time t; the height reached is h = g·t²/8. It's the method used by contact mats and the My Jump app. Tremors that are too short and thrown phones (over a second of flight) are rejected.
